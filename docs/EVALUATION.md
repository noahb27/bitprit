# Evaluation protocol

Declare five ascending UTC dates before fitting. The intervals are training, calibration, model selection, and a sealed later holdout. A market's full open-to-close window must belong to one interval. A finalized result must have been received before that interval ends. Do not explore the holdout with replay or dashboard tools before grading the frozen challenger. The registry refuses to grade different challengers on overlapping holdouts. After using a holdout, declare a fresh later holdout for the next model.

## Targets

- **Settlement** estimates the calibrated probability of an official finalized Yes. The label comes from the exact Kalshi market and event. Coinbase returns, distance to the published strike, the market midpoint, depth and time remaining are features. Kalshi supplies the outcome. Spot direction alone does not define the label. The basis diagnostic compares a sufficiently sampled final-minute Coinbase mean with Kalshi's reported expiration value where available.
- **Resale** forecasts the distribution of the full-size executable bid and net dollars per contract after both legs, separately for Yes and No at 30, 60 and 120 seconds. Simulate the first eligible entry after latency, then the first eligible exit after the horizon measured from the entry fill and a second latency. Feature time and executable entry time are distinct. Missing, thin or late bids and continuity gaps are censored with explicit reasons. P10, P50 and P90 are conditional on a full round trip. Report fill coverage alongside model errors. The fixed-horizon label ignores price-triggered early exits, which are graded separately as policy simulation.

The training sample is the first feature-ready observation within 20 seconds of minutes 2, 5, 8 and 11. Fitting and scoring weight each market equally. All features use responses already received at the signal. Models use trailing spot samples with measured continuity. The current model does not consume candles. The settlement candidates are scaled logistic regression and a depth-four tree with separate calibration on earlier days. Each resale target compares scaled ridge and a depth-four tree. Calibration uses held calibration residuals to construct the predictive intervals. Selection uses the next interval for Brier or mean absolute error of the median forecast. Baselines include the training base rate, market midpoint, unchanged executable bid, no trade and a fixed cheap-contract rule.

## Gates fixed before holdout grading

- Fitting settlement requires at least 24, 10 and 10 labeled markets in the training, calibration and selection periods. Each period needs at least two UTC days and both outcomes. Each side and resale horizon needs at least 20, 8 and 8 labeled markets over the same phases, at least two days in each, and 60% round-trip label coverage among executable signals.
- Holdout scoring requires at least 16 settlement markets with both outcomes and at least 12 markets per resale head, each over at least three UTC days. Each resale head needs 60% label coverage.
- Compare settlement-only, resale-only, and combined policies on the same holdout timeline, using common execution and budget rules. The settlement policy holds to official settlement. The resale policy requires P10 net return above the minimum edge. The combined policy also requires the weighted sum of 80% resale P10 and 20% buffered settlement value net of entry cost to beat that edge. A favorable short-term sale can clear those checks even if settlement is unlikely.
- A forward trial requires the settlement Brier score to beat both baselines. Each resale net head must match or beat its unchanged-bid absolute error and achieve at least 65% coverage of its nominal 80% interval. The combined policy needs at least 12 closed trades with no remaining open position, net P&L above no-trade and the fixed rule, and a positive lower bound from the UTC-day bootstrap of mean daily P&L.
- Promotion requires an explicit command and eligible forward trial results recorded after holdout grading. Require at least 20 fully closed trial trades across five UTC days with no open position or pending order, positive trial net P&L and a positive day-bootstrap lower bound. Keep gathering data when estimates are inconclusive. The bootstrap intervals describe variation across the days observed. Small or correlated samples limit what those intervals can establish.

Prices and grade metrics are experimental research inputs. Simulations use sampled books, additive slippage and a per-level rounded taker fee estimate. Track missing fills, costs, exposure, drawdown and concentration alongside gains. The software tests use synthetic observations. Evidence about forecast accuracy requires official held-out outcomes. Evidence about paper results requires timestamped executable depth and forward coverage. Profitability in actual trading has not been measured.
